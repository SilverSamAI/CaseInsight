# SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

## 1.1 The core finding

Silverside's own closed and active revenue is the most reliable ICP evidence available, and it points somewhere more specific than "enterprises that want AI content."

**[FACT]** Silverside's realized and in flight commercial record includes Sam's Club (retail, ISB retainer, Phase 2 at $350K), IHG (hospitality, two SOWs closed), Keurig Dr Pepper (CPG, $198.5K POC scoped), Mattress Firm (retail, $75K AI Content Engine), Smoothie King (QSR franchise, approximately $2.46M proposed), Supersure ($1.08M retainer), ResMed (medical device, $125K to $135K TVC), Chime (consumer fintech, $146K AI TVC), Carlsberg (beverage, cold sourced, NDA executed), Nacelle (entertainment IP, animation retainer), Medical Guardian (consumer health), and Marc Jacobs and Benefit Cosmetics (fashion and beauty inside the LVMH orbit).

**[INFERENCE, High]** The unifying variable across that list is not industry. It is **content multiplication load**: the number of assets a company must produce equals SKUs times channels times markets times seasons times audience segments. Every account above has a multiplier problem that traditional production cannot serve economically.

**[INFERENCE, High]** The second unifying variable is **a named executive holding a cost, speed, or AI mandate**. Silverside sells against a mandate, not against a budget line. Where no mandate exists, the deal stalls at pilot regardless of company size.

**[FACT]** The outbound audit found the ICP was targeting off charter verticals, with Financial Services at 36 percent of targeting volume. This playbook resolves that: regulated institutional financial services is Tier 4, while **consumer fintech and challenger banks with consumer brand advertising budgets are Tier 2**, which is why Chime closed at $146K and why the segment must be defined by media behavior rather than by SIC code.

## 1.2 One sentence ICP

> A North American consumer facing brand, retailer, or multi brand portfolio between **$250M and $10B in revenue**, with a **SKU, market, or franchise multiplication problem**, **$5M or more in annual working media**, **$2M or more in existing agency and production spend that can be reallocated**, and a **named executive carrying an AI, cost reduction, or speed to market mandate**.

## 1.3 One paragraph ICP

Silverside's ideal customer is a consumer facing enterprise or upper mid market organization, headquartered or with a major marketing centre in North America or the UK, generating between $250M and $10B in annual revenue, operating a portfolio of at least three brands or at least 500 revenue relevant SKUs or at least 100 locations or markets. It spends $5M or more on working media and $2M or more annually on external creative and production, and it experiences a measurable production bottleneck: campaign turnaround measured in weeks rather than days, single digit variant counts on seven figure media buys, per asset costs in the thousands, and a backlog of channel and regional requests its in house studio cannot absorb. It has a marketing, brand, creative, digital, or innovation executive who has been publicly or internally tasked with reducing marketing cost, increasing content velocity, or operationalizing AI, and it has enough brand governance need that consumer grade generative tools have already failed an internal pilot. It can supply brand guidelines, product imagery, and legal parameters within 30 days, and it has budget authority at director level or above to authorize a $75K to $250K first engagement without a full enterprise procurement cycle.

## 1.9 Top reasons an account IS a strong fit

1. A quantified production bottleneck the buyer can state in numbers
2. Three or more brands, or high SKU count, or multi market or multi location footprint
3. A named executive with a cost, speed, or AI mandate
4. Existing external production spend of $2M or more that can be reallocated rather than newly funded
5. Ability to supply brand guidelines, product imagery, and legal parameters within 30 days
6. Director level or above authority to fund $75K to $250K without full enterprise procurement
7. A prior failed or underwhelming internal AI pilot, which proves intent and reveals the governance gap
8. Repeatable seasonal or drop based calendar, which makes recurring revenue structural rather than negotiated

## 1.10 Top reasons an account is NOT a fit

1. Content volume low enough that per asset economics never justify system build
2. No budget owner in the conversation after two calls
3. Restrictive enterprise AI policy with no exception pathway, common in regulated banking, insurance, and pharma
4. Procurement burden disproportionate to deal size, typically enterprise vendor onboarding for a $50K pilot
5. Seeking commodity execution at commodity price rather than a system
6. No brand assets, no product imagery, no guidelines, so there is nothing to train
7. Purely exploratory interest with no problem statement and no timeline
8. Expectation that AI means free or near free

## 3.2 Tier assignments and rationale

### TIER 1: Immediate sales priority, 60 to 70 percent of cold outbound volume

Retail and mass merchant, CPG multi brand, Beauty and personal care, Food and beverage, Restaurants and QSR and franchise, Fashion and apparel and footwear, E-commerce and DTC.

**Rationale:** Every Tier 1 vertical satisfies all four gating conditions simultaneously: high content multiplication, a Silverside reference client the buyer will recognize, a marketing budget that is discretionary at director or VP level, and a governance need that consumer grade tools cannot meet. **[FACT]** Silverside has live proof in six of the seven. **[INFERENCE, High]** These verticals also align to the five locked mid market verticals, so no strategy conflict exists.

### TIER 2: Strong opportunity, 25 to 30 percent of volume

Consumer health, Medical device, Hospitality, Marketplaces and retail media, Consumer electronics, Automotive, Consumer fintech, Travel, Media and entertainment IP, Alcohol, Fitness, Pet, Home goods, Agencies as partner, Recently funded tech.

**Rationale:** Each has a strong multiplier and a real budget, but one gating condition is weaker. Consumer health and medical device carry longer regulatory review. Automotive and consumer electronics carry longer procurement. Recently funded tech has weak expansion certainty. Agencies compress margin. **Work these deliberately, not as filler.**

### TIER 3: Selective and opportunistic, 5 to 10 percent of volume

Gaming, Sports, B2B SaaS, Adtech and martech, Telecom, Nonprofit, Education.

**Rationale:** **[FACT]** Silverside has approved references in B2B tech (CyberArk, SimpliSafe) and nonprofit (Goodwill), which proves the work is deliverable. But content multiplication is structurally lower, deal sizes are smaller, and expansion is limited. **Pursue only on a specific trigger**: a $20M or more raise, a new CMO, or a category launch. Never build a standing sequence here.

### TIER 4: Low priority or poor fit, 0 percent of cold outbound

Insurance, Pharma, Institutional financial services, Healthcare providers and payers, Industrial B2B, Government, Professional services, Real estate, Crypto.

**Rationale:** **[FACT]** The audit found Financial Services at 36 percent of targeting, which is the single largest misallocation in the current program. The blocker is not fit in the abstract, it is enterprise AI policy, MLR or compliance review cycles, and procurement cost that exceeds first deal value. **[FACT]** Chime and Fifth Third Bank are real Silverside relationships, which is why this needs a precise rule rather than a blanket exclusion:

> **THE FINANCIAL SERVICES RULE.** A financial services company enters the cold universe only if it advertises like a consumer brand: consumer challenger bank, neobank, consumer payments, or consumer fintech, with 50 or more active creatives in Meta Ad Library and a CMO or VP Brand who owns a working media budget. Everything else, meaning commercial banking, wealth management, institutional asset management, insurance carriers, and regional retail banking, is excluded from cold outbound and handled warm or partner led only.

Apply the identical test to pharma: consumer OTC and consumer health brands inside a pharma parent are Tier 2, while Rx and clinical are Tier 4.

## 5.3 Deep dive on the eight highest value use cases

### UC1: Campaign asset variation and adaptation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Business problem** | A campaign is built once at high cost, then needs 50 to 500 derivative versions across channels, formats, aspect ratios, languages, offers, and audiences. Derivative work consumes 60 to 80 percent of production budget while creating none of the creative value |
| **Owning department** | Creative Operations, with Brand Marketing as the funder |
| **Economic buyer** | VP Brand Marketing or VP Creative Operations |
| **Day to day user** | Creative operations manager, production coordinator, studio designer |
| **Technical stakeholders** | DAM administrator, marketing technology lead |
| **Required inputs** | Master campaign assets, brand guidelines, channel spec sheet, approved copy variants, legal parameters |
| **Expected outputs** | Full derivative asset set across every specified channel, format, market, and audience, with version control and approval trail |
| **Time to value** | **2 to 4 weeks.** Fastest of any use case |
| **Success metrics** | Cost per derivative asset, turnaround time from master approval to full set delivery, number of variants shipped per campaign, percent of requests fulfilled on time |
| **Estimated project value** | $60K to $200K per campaign program. $250K to $900K annualized |
| **Recurring revenue potential** | **Very high.** Every campaign generates the same need |
| **Expansion** | Into localization, then into modular system build, then into Brand Brain |
| **Implementation complexity** | **Low.** No systems integration required for v1. Assets in, assets out |

## 6.3 Multi threading strategy for enterprise accounts

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Target **four to six contacts per enterprise account** across three functional layers. Single threaded enterprise deals stall when the one contact changes role, and **[FACT]** in a $250M or more organization the average tenure of a marketing VP is short enough that this is a real and frequent risk.

| Layer | Who | Purpose | Sequence timing | Message emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Layer 1: Operator** | Creative Ops, Marketing Ops, Production, E-commerce content | Find and quantify the pain. Build the internal business case | **Day 1.** Enter here first | Throughput, backlog, cost per asset, SLA |
| **Layer 2: Budget** | VP Brand, VP Digital, VP E-commerce, VP Field Marketing | Fund the pilot | **Day 3 to 5.** Slight offset from Layer 1 | More versions per budget, speed to market, revenue impact |
| **Layer 3: Sponsor** | CMO, CDO, CBO, Chief AI Officer | Authorize scale and remove blockers | **Day 10 to 14**, or immediately if a trigger is fresh | Peer benchmark, cost structure, competitive position |
| **Layer 4: Validator** | MarTech, Innovation, Head of Gen AI | De risk technically | On request, or day 14 if Layer 1 to 3 are cold | Architecture, governance, integration, honest limits |
| **Layer 5: Gate** | Procurement, Legal, InfoSec | Close paperwork | **Only after verbal.** Never cold | Standard MSA, insurance, security documentation, AI governance framework |

**Rules:**
1. Never contact more than two people at an account in the same 48 hours. Coordinated volume reads as a blast.
2. Vary the angle by persona. Identical copy to three people at one company is the fastest way to get flagged internally.
3. If Layer 1 replies, **pause Layers 2 and 3 immediately.** Let the champion route you internally. Going over their head after they engaged is the most common self inflicted enterprise loss.
4. If nobody replies after the full sequence, add a second cohort of contacts from adjacent functions before writing the account off.
5. **[STANDING INSTRUCTION]** All cold outbound runs on Apollo cousin domains. HubSpot is warm contacts only. Human approval required before any send.

## 16.5 Total priority score and tiering

```
FIT       = Section 16.1, 0 to 100, times strategic multiplier 1.0 to 1.3
INTENT    = Section 16.2, 0 to 50, time decayed
RELATION  = Section 16.3, 0 to 25
OPPTY     = Section 16.4, 0 to 25

TOTAL PRIORITY = (FIT x 0.45) + (INTENT x 0.25) + (RELATION x 0.10) + (OPPTY x 0.20)
                 normalized to a 0 to 100 scale
```

| Total priority | Tier | Action | Contacts per account | Research investment | Sequence type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **80 to 100** | **Tier 1: Named** | Account plan, executive engagement, immediate multi thread | 5 to 8 | **60 to 90 minutes per account** | Fully bespoke, hand written |
| **65 to 79** | **Tier 2: Priority** | Personalized multi thread sequence | 4 to 6 | 20 to 30 minutes | Vertical template plus 2 personalized lines per email |
| **50 to 64** | **Tier 3: Volume** | Segmented sequence | 2 to 3 | **Zero. Do not research** | Vertical template plus 1 merge personalized line |
| **35 to 49** | **Nurture** | Marketing owned. No sequence | 1 | Zero | Content and event only |
| **Under 35** | **Disqualify** | Suppress. Log reason | 0 | Zero | None |

**Interaction rules:**

1. **FIT is the gate, INTENT is the timer.** A FIT under 45 never enters a sequence regardless of INTENT. A high INTENT on a low FIT account is a distraction, and **[FACT]** this is exactly the mechanism that produced 36 percent Financial Services targeting.
2. **INTENT determines sequencing order within a tier.** Two accounts with FIT 75 are worked in INTENT order.
3. **RELATION overrides channel.** A RELATION of 15 or more routes to warm outreach in HubSpot, not to a cold Apollo sequence. **[STANDING INSTRUCTION]** HubSpot is warm only, Apollo handles cold. **A high RELATION account must never enter a cold cousin domain sequence.**
4. **OPPTY determines effort allocation, not entry.** A Tier 2 account with OPPTY 22 deserves executive involvement earlier than a Tier 2 account with OPPTY 8.
5. **Recompute INTENT weekly. Recompute FIT quarterly or on a material event.** FIT is structural and slow moving. INTENT is perishable.

# SECTION 23: TARGET ACCOUNT EXAMPLES

The full 100 account target list is delivered as a separate scored, Apollo ready, HubSpot importable workbook. It is organized by vertical, revenue band, tier, and use case, with per account fit rationale, target titles, trigger hypotheses flagged for verification, and the approved reference client to cite.

**Evidence discipline for the account list, applied throughout:**

- **[FACT]** columns: company name, industry, headquarters, ownership type, approximate revenue band, brand and portfolio structure, and location or SKU scale where publicly observable
- **[INFERENCE]** columns: fit rationale, primary use case, target titles, and estimated deal band
- **[HYPOTHESIS, requires verification]** columns: trigger hypothesis and multiplier estimates

**No account in the list is asserted to have a specific problem.** Fit rationale describes structural characteristics that make a problem likely, and the workbook contains a dedicated `verification_required` column naming what must be confirmed before the account enters a sequence.

1. **Agency review, AOR termination, or in housing announcement.** Budget is in motion and a decision is already being made.

4. **PE acquisition within 24 months.** EBITDA mandate creates urgency no other trigger matches.
5. **Public cost reduction or marketing efficiency mandate.** An executive has committed to a number they now have to hit.

1. **Localization and market versioning.** Highest revenue, repeatability, and scalability. The expansion engine.
2. **Campaign asset variation and adaptation.** Highest urgency plus ease of sale plus speed to value. **The cold entry wedge.**
3. **Franchise and location level localization.** Structurally recurring by design.
4. **E-commerce and PDP content at scale.** Clearest ROI math and highest raw volume.
